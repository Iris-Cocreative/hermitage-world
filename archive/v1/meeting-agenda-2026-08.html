Suggested additions to the moodboard

Images worth gathering before or during the session — each one anchors a part of the vision we named in July, and together they'd help the board see the direction rather than hear it described.

The architecture of light

  • Ronchamp's south wall (Le Corbusier, Notre-Dame du Haut) — the scattered, deep-set colored-glass apertures that directly inspired the prototype's signature element
  • Breuer's St. John's Abbey Church (Collegeville, MN) — board-formed concrete as a sacred material, and a honeycomb window wall that's stained glass without one tradition's iconography
  • The Indianapolis Airport Civic Plaza atrium — Garry's own image from the vision session: vast glass-and-beam space, awe without religiosity. It belongs on the board in his words

World sacred geometry

  • One image each from the traditions Samuel named — a Timurid dome interior (Samarkand), an Ottoman courtyard (Sinan), a Ming temple hall — chosen for shared proportion and symmetry rather than ornament, making the interfaith breadth visible
  • Carved Roman capitals — an inscription cut in stone (Trajan's Column lettering) — the root of the site's Marcellus typography and "letters carved into concrete" feel

The Franciscan ground note

  • The architecture of a forest — light shafting through a high canopy, trunks as columns — Samuel's "geometry meeting organic form," and the counterweight that keeps the concrete from feeling cold
  • Humble warm materials — rough-sawn wood, undyed wool or linen weave (a nod to the tapestry), a simple rope cincture — Franciscan simplicity as texture, not symbol
  • The Hermitage's own artifacts — even phone photos of the stickman stained glass, the companion nun piece, and the tapestry would ground the board in the real lineage while we wait on Garry's high-res versions

Material and light studies

  • Raking light on plaster — morning sun across a rough warm wall, the texture of the site's light grounds
  • Amber glass lit from behind — a single glowing pane in a dark room, the palette's one accent as a physical object
  • A threshold — a heavy door standing open onto light, or a vestibule between dark and bright — the "facade, door, hallways" metaphor in one frame

Just as useful: a small "not this" corner — a lotus logo, a sage-green gradient, a stock sunset meditation photo — to make the deliberate refusal of new-age defaults explicit for the board.
