The quiet space within

Meditation is how you find it. Friar Justin taught that it is more than relaxation, stress relief or quieting the mind: it is a way of discovering who we are, and of experiencing the Divine Presence within us.

Two practices, one journey

The journey into awareness has two complementary sides: active meditation, and contemplation, which Justin also called passive meditation.

Active meditation

Discovering our power to change

We consciously direct our thoughts and imagination toward growth. Through visualization we explore new possibilities, develop positive qualities, clarify our intentions, and form an inner picture of the life we want to live. It moves us from habitual patterns toward intentional living.

“Taking control of the thought process and giving it direction.”

Friar Justin Success Thinking, ch. III
Contemplation

Discovering who we are in the quiet

Rather than directing our thoughts toward an outcome, we enter the quiet with openness and curiosity, and learn to listen: to our deeper selves, to the wisdom that rises from within, and to the Divine Presence that is always with us. It is not the absence of activity. It is the practice of becoming deeply present.

“In Passive Mental Prayer there is no language. It is simply ‘being’ with God.”

Friar Justin Success Thinking, ch. III

Active meditation helps us discover what we can become. Contemplation helps us discover who we are.

A first sitting

Ten minutes is enough. Nothing here asks for belief; everything invites practice.

“Meditation is to the mind as exercise is to the body. In a world where your attention has become the product, learning to strengthen and use it is a necessity.”

Samuel Robinson Program Director

  1. Settle

    Sit upright and at ease, in a chair or on a cushion. Let your hands rest. Close your eyes or let them soften.

  2. Relax

    Breathe a little slower than usual. Let the body loosen from the top of the head down to the feet.

  3. Rest

    Let your attention rest on the breath, or on one quiet word. When thoughts arrive, notice them without argument.

  4. Return

    Come back to the breath as often as you need to. Returning is the practice. Close with a moment of thanks.
